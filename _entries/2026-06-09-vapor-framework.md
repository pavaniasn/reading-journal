summary: |
  VAPOR is a scored self-assessment framework for students using AI in writing. Each letter names one dimension: Voice (does this sound like you?), Agency (did you drive the AI, or did it drive you?), Posture (are you positioning yourself for your audience or just performing "expected"?), Origin (can you trace your ideas and language choices?), Responsibility (can you defend this as your own work?). Counterintuitively, a HIGH score means uncritical AI use — the metaphor being that vapor lacks weight, presence, and authenticity. The tool is designed for classroom use, adaptable across disciplines, with reflection prompts after each scored question.
notes: |
  What struck me is that VAPOR addresses the learner's relationship to AI output — not AI knowledge, not technical skill, but *authorial integrity under AI influence*. That is a genuinely different construct from what most AI literacy frameworks measure.

  My idea: develop a parallel framework specifically for *educators and program designers* — not for writing, but for *how they design, deploy, and interpret AI literacy assessments*. The VAPOR frame maps neatly onto the gap I'm seeing in my own survey work: respondents can score high on AI knowledge items and still have no real agency over how AI shapes their decisions or their students' learning. A framework that names those dimensions explicitly — and is structured as a self-assessment appendix — could make the AI literacy survey feel complete rather than just diagnostic.

  The key design principle from VAPOR worth keeping: high score = problem, not achievement. That inversion is pedagogically powerful because it forces reflection rather than reassurance.

  *(Claude suggestion, not mine yet)* Possible working name: TRACE (Transparency, Responsibility, Agency, Critical awareness, Epistemic grounding) — or something entirely different once the dimensions are clearer.

  Next step: draft the dimensions for this companion framework alongside the survey instrument and see if they hold.
